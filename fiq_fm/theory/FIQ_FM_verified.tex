\title{Gauge-Fixed Flow-Induced Quotient Flow Matching\\
\large Full-Density Endpoint Fidelity with Low-Dimensional Iterative Dynamics}
\author{Verified theory, algorithm, adversarial audit, and experiments}
\date{16 August 2026}
\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A latent flow is efficient because its expensive iterative vector field acts on a low-dimensional state, but the usual variational-autoencoder tokenizer can discard density, spend rate matching a fixed prior, and choose a code for reconstruction rather than for closure of the generative dynamics.  A full ambient flow avoids that bottleneck but repeatedly processes the ambient dimension.  We give a complete affirmative result on a realistic structural class and a falsifiable algorithm outside that class.  The method, Gauge-Fixed Flow-Induced Quotient Flow Matching (GF-FIQ), learns a static orthogonal quotient directly from ordinary flow-matching labels, fixes the otherwise unidentified residual coordinate gauge by a cross-fitted conditional-dependence graph, integrates a deep flow only in the active coordinates, and samples a one-shot stochastic block fiber for the complementary randomness.  For triangular transports with block-local Gaussian fibers, GF-FIQ reproduces the exact full-dimensional endpoint law, has iterative complexity governed by the active dimension, and strictly dominates a same-dimensional KL-VAE with a diagonal residual decoder by an explicit conditional KL gap.  We also prove raw-label subspace recovery, exact loss isometry, exact density-error decomposition, pair-block gauge recovery, finite-sample subspace and model-selection bounds, and a Wasserstein perturbation bound under approximate projectability.  Five-seed experiments support every promoted claim: on an exact $D=18,d=2$ quotient, GF-FIQ improves held-out sliced Wasserstein error over a parameter-matched full flow, KL-VAE latent flow, and representation-autoencoder latent flow, while its learned block-versus-diagonal likelihood gain matches the analytic $1.291$-nat gap.  On real $8\times8$ digits with $d=16$, it significantly improves held-out sliced Wasserstein and conditional-label fidelity over both latent baselines, although its feature-Fr\'echet advantage over the strongest baseline is not statistically established.  The result is conditional rather than universal: arbitrary full flows need not admit cheap quotients, deterministic $d<D$ decoders cannot match full-dimensional densities, and the present dense chart does not by itself prove image-scale asymptotic speedups.
\end{abstract}
