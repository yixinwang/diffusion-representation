\section{Finite-sample and approximation bounds}

The following results separate estimation error in the active field from endpoint error and model-selection uncertainty.

\subsection{Active field oracle inequality}

Let $\cU_d$ be a bounded active-field class, and let $B_z=Q_z^\top V$.  Define the approximation error
\begin{equation}
 A_d^2=\inf_{u\in\cU_d}\E\norm{B_z-u(T,Z,C)}^2-\E\norm{B_z-u_d^*(T,Z,C)}^2,
\end{equation}
where $u_d^*=\E[B_z\mid T,Z,C]$ is unrestricted.  For empirical risk minimization $\wh u_d$ under bounded squared loss, a standard Rademacher argument gives, with probability at least $1-\alpha$,
\begin{equation}
 \E\norm{\wh u_d-u_d^*}^2
 \lesssim A_d^2+\mathfrak R_n(\ell\circ\cU_d)+B^2\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\alpha)}{n}}.
 \label{eq:oracle}
\end{equation}
The scientific content is not the generic empirical-process inequality; it is that $A_d$ is the observable closure boundary.  If $A_d$ remains large, the claimed quotient is false at that dimension.

\subsection{Endpoint perturbation}

Let $Y_t=(Z_t,R_t)$ follow the exact structured coordinate field $a_t^*$ and let $\wh Y_t$ follow $\wh a_t$.  Suppose both use the same source, $\wh a_t$ is $L$-Lipschitz uniformly in time, both solutions have finite second moments, and
\begin{equation}
 \int_0^1\E\norm{\wh a_t(Y_t)-a_t^*(Y_t)}^2dt\le\varepsilon^2.
 \label{eq:field-error}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Wasserstein stability]
Under the preceding assumptions and exact integration,
\begin{equation}
 W_2(\cL(\wh X_1),\cL(X_1))
 \le e^L\varepsilon,
 \label{eq:w2}
\end{equation}
where $X=QY$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Couple the solutions through the same source and write $\Delta_t=\wh Y_t-Y_t$.  The identity
\[
 \dot\Delta_t=\wh a_t(\wh Y_t)-\wh a_t(Y_t)+\wh a_t(Y_t)-a_t^*(Y_t)
\]
uses the Lipschitz assumption on $\wh a_t$.  Gr\"onwall and Cauchy--Schwarz in time give $\{\E\norm{\Delta_1}^2\}^{1/2}\le e^L\varepsilon$.  Orthogonality preserves Euclidean distance, and this coupling upper bounds $W_2$.
\end{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:w2} uses regularity of $\wh a_t$ and the true-path error in \eqref{eq:field-error}.  A bound estimated under another path additionally needs a change-of-measure control.

\subsection{Dimension and block selection}

Let $\wh R_d$ be held-out active-plus-fiber loss, let $\operatorname{pen}_n(d,b)$ be a capacity penalty, and let $\operatorname{cost}(d,b)$ include measured FLOPs or time.  Select
\begin{equation}
 (\wh d,\wh b)=\argmin_{d,b}
 \left\{\wh R_d(b)+\operatorname{pen}_n(d,b)+\lambda\operatorname{cost}(d,b)\right\}.
 \label{eq:model-select}
\end{equation}
A finite candidate-set union bound yields an oracle inequality against the best candidate up to twice the uniform validation deviation.  This formalizes the bias--variance--compute tradeoff; it does not manufacture a low-dimensional quotient when no candidate has small held-out defect.

\section{Experimental protocol}

The protocol fixes information, held-out use, metrics, and cost accounting before any archived or fresh result is interpreted.

\subsection{Fairness and leakage controls}

Every seed is split before fitting.  Standardization, chart estimation, fixed-axis residual partitioning, early stopping, VAE/RAE encoders, decoders, linear-probe tuning, and evaluator selection use only train and validation data.  Test data are used once for final metrics.  All generative methods receive the same examples, labels, active dimension, sample count, and Heun NFE.  FIQ, VAE-LFM, and RAE-LFM use the same active vector-field architecture.  The full ambient width is chosen so its trainable generation parameters do not exceed the FIQ generation budget.  Endpoint decoder/fiber and chart time are included.

The five recorded distribution metrics are sliced $W_2$, an adaptive-bandwidth Gaussian-kernel MMD U-statistic clipped at zero, energy distance, covariance error, and normalized mean error.  Digits additionally use a train-only feature/classifier network to report feature Fr\'echet, class-conditional feature Fr\'echet, feature-space precision/recall, and requested-label accuracy.  Because the old digits seeds reuse the same finite dataset, their resampling intervals describe split sensitivity rather than independent scientific replication.

\section{Failure trace and adversarial audit}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Residual-basis boundary:} the fixed-axis graph does not recover a generic rotation.  The commutant-spectral route does so only under response-rank, irreducibility, inequivalence, and eigengap assumptions.
\item \textbf{Benchmark limitation:} the archived synthetic benchmark has unequal residual marginal variances and tests pair partitioning, not rotation recovery.  The new tied-marginal Haar confirmation passes NLL and leakage gates but fails projector and per-unit response-rank gates.
\item \textbf{Support check:} every deterministic $d<D$ proposal was rejected for full-density parity.
\item \textbf{Compute check:} the chart and fiber are included in timing.  The dense chart prevents an image-scale asymptotic claim.
\item \textbf{Baseline check:} removing the VAE KL penalty and giving the RAE a block stochastic decoder produces a materially stronger baseline; it is retained.
\item \textbf{Metric check:} digits feature Fr\'echet is not declared significantly better when the paired interval includes zero.
\item \textbf{Information check:} the same labels and splits are supplied to every method; no group or test labels enter model selection.
\item \textbf{Proof boundary:} exact pair matching is proved; greedy blocks of size four on digits are not.
\end{enumerate}
The detailed registry and audit are stored with the code in \texttt{theory/approach\_registry.md} and \texttt{theory/adversarial\_audit.md}.

\section{What is and is not established}

\paragraph{Established.}
On $\mathfrak T(d,b)$, the algorithm has exact full-dimensional endpoint law under realizability and exact integration, exact latent/fiber KL decomposition, and iterative dynamics only in dimension $d$.  Raw flow labels recover the active subspace in the spiked linear-Gaussian regime.  Generic two-block residual rotations are identifiable under the response-rank and commutant assumptions above; pair partitions on fixed axes are identifiable under a matching margin.  A diagonal stochastic VAE decoder has the explicit strict gap \eqref{eq:diag-gap}.  The first residual-rotation confirmation fails structural recovery, so the empirical rotation mechanism is not established.

\paragraph{Not established.}
There is no universal cheap quotient, no universal dominance over an unrestricted VAE, no proof that the greedy block-four grouping is optimal, and no demonstrated parity with a converged full pixel flow on modern image benchmarks.  A structured reversible image chart, convolutional or tokenized block fiber, compute-matched DiT baselines, and image-scale FID/likelihood experiments are required for that next claim.

\section{Conclusion}

The mathematically coherent route is not to compress the endpoint and hope that a low-dimensional code happens to be generatively sufficient.  It is to identify a quotient of the full generative law, preserve complementary randomness, and spend iterative computation only where the transport is genuinely nonlinear.  A correct active subspace can still yield an inefficient fiber in the wrong residual basis.  Conditional covariance contrasts can register that basis when their response span and commutant are identifiable; otherwise the algorithm must abstain or pay for a richer fiber.  Conditional on an aligned block-local stochastic fiber, the model gives exact full-density parity on its declared class and a strict separation from diagonal VAE decoders.  Image-scale claims require a structured chart, a small held-out quotient defect, a cheap fiber, successful registered residual-basis recovery, and compute-matched full-flow quality.
