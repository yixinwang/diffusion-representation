\section{Learning the quotient from raw flow labels}

The first identification result treats a static linear chart; the residual-basis problem is then separated from active-subspace recovery.

\subsection{Population moment identity}

Assume for this subsection that $\E X_1=0$, $\Cov(X_1)=\Sigma$, and $t\in(0,1)$ is fixed.  From \eqref{eq:rectified},
\begin{align}
 \E[VX_t^\top]
 &=\E[(X_1-X_0)((1-t)X_0+tX_1)^\top]\nonumber\\
 &=t\Sigma-(1-t)I_D.
 \label{eq:moment}
\end{align}
Thus the raw flow-label moment has exactly the same eigenvectors as the data covariance.

\begin{theorem}[Active-subspace recovery]
Suppose
\begin{equation}
 \Sigma=Q_z\Lambda_zQ_z^\top+\lambda_r Q_rQ_r^\top,
 \quad \min_j(\Lambda_{z,jj}-\lambda_r)=\delta>0.
 \label{eq:spike}
\end{equation}
Then the leading $d$ eigenspace of $M_t=\E\operatorname{sym}(VX_t^\top)$ is $\operatorname{span}(Q_z)$, with eigengap $t\delta$.  If $\norm{\wh M_t-M_t}_{\mathrm{op}}\le\eta<t\delta/2$, then
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\sin\Theta(\wh Q_z,Q_z)}_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\frac{2\eta}{t\delta}.
 \label{eq:dk}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:moment} is a scalar affine transformation of $\Sigma$, so it preserves eigenvectors and multiplies covariance eigengaps by $t$.  Apply the Davis--Kahan sine theorem.
\end{proof}

For sub-Gaussian samples, matrix Bernstein or standard covariance concentration gives $\eta=O_{\Pp}(K^2\sqrt{(D+\log(1/\alpha))/n})$ up to the usual lower-order term.  The moment needs one sampled path time and one outer product, or a randomized sketch, per observation; no ambient neural vector field is fitted.

\subsection{The residual gauge}

The active theorem does \emph{not} identify the basis of $\operatorname{span}(Q_r)$ when the residual eigenvalues are tied.  If $O\in\R^{(D-d)\times(D-d)}$ is orthogonal, then $[Q_z,Q_rO]$ recovers the same active quotient.  A cheap block covariance in one residual basis can become dense after rotation by $O$.

The original conditional-dependence graph does not solve this rotation problem.  It scores a residual basis after it has been fixed and only permutes those axes into blocks.  A second registered route uses conditional covariance contrasts to recover an unordered block decomposition.  Matrix joint block diagonalization is prior work \cite{cai-li-2021}; the contribution needed here is the connection between cross-fitted conditional moments, a symmetric commutant, and the residual gauge.

\subsection{Commutant-spectral block registration}

Write $q=D-d$ and let $\mathbb S^q_0$ be the traceless symmetric $q\times q$ matrices.  Suppose centered conditional residual covariance has contrast span
\begin{equation}
 C_\ell=O\,\diag(C_\ell^{(1)},C_\ell^{(2)})O^\top,
 \qquad \ell=1,\ldots,L,
 \label{eq:rotation-contrasts}
\end{equation}
for an unknown orthogonal $O$ and two blocks of size two.  Define the commutant quadratic form
\begin{equation}
 \ip{H}{\mathcal G H}
 =\sum_{\ell=1}^L\norm{C_\ell H-HC_\ell}_{F}^{2},
 \qquad H\in\mathbb S^4_0.
 \label{eq:commutant-gram}
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}[Irreducible inequivalent residual blocks]
Within each block, the only matrix commuting with every $C_\ell^{(j)}$ is a scalar multiple of $I_2$.  There is no nonzero $X\in\R^{2\times2}$ satisfying $C_\ell^{(1)}X=XC_\ell^{(2)}$ for every $\ell$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Residual block registration]
Under the irreducible-inequivalent assumption, the kernel of $\mathcal G$ on $\mathbb S^4_0$ is one-dimensional and is spanned by
\begin{equation}
 H^*=\frac12 O\,\diag(I_2,-I_2)O^\top.
 \label{eq:separator}
\end{equation}
Consequently the positive and negative eigenspaces of $H^*$ recover the two residual blocks up to within-block orthogonal transformations and a block swap.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Conjugate a symmetric commutant by $O$.  Its diagonal blocks commute with every within-block contrast and hence are scalar by irreducibility.  Its off-diagonal block is an intertwiner and hence vanishes by inequivalence.  Tracelessness leaves only matrices proportional to $\diag(I_2,-I_2)$.  The two eigenspaces of \eqref{eq:separator} are precisely the registered blocks.
\end{proof}

The contrast span is estimated without applying an SVD to feature-regression coefficients.  Let $\operatorname{vec}_s$ be an isometry from symmetric matrices to Euclidean vectors.  Two regressions fitted on disjoint training halves give centered fitted moments $\wh m_1(Z)$ and $\wh m_2(Z)$.  The algorithm diagonalizes the symmetrized response operator
\begin{equation}
 \wh{\mathcal R}
 =\frac12\wh\E\!\left[
 \operatorname{vec}_s(\wh m_1)\operatorname{vec}_s(\wh m_2)^\top+
 \operatorname{vec}_s(\wh m_2)\operatorname{vec}_s(\wh m_1)^\top
 \right].
 \label{eq:cross-response}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Population response-span recovery]
If the population fitted moment can be written as
$m_\phi(Z)=\sum_{\ell=1}^L g_\ell(Z)C_\ell$,
the contrast vectors $\operatorname{vec}_s(C_\ell)$ are linearly independent, and
$\E[g(Z)g(Z)^\top]$ is positive definite, then the range of the population version of \eqref{eq:cross-response} is exactly
$\operatorname{span}\{\operatorname{vec}_s(C_\ell)\}_{\ell=1}^L$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $A$ have columns $\operatorname{vec}_s(C_\ell)$ and
$\Gamma=\E[g(Z)g(Z)^\top]$.  The response operator is
$A\Gamma A^\top$.  Full column rank of $A$ and positive definiteness of $\Gamma$ give
$\operatorname{range}(A\Gamma A^\top)=\operatorname{range}(A)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite commutant perturbation]
Let $\delta=\lambda_2(\mathcal G)>0$ be the commutant eigengap on $\mathbb S^4_0$ and suppose
$\norm{\wh{\mathcal G}-\mathcal G}_{\mathrm{op}}\le\varepsilon\le\delta/32$.
Let $\wh P$ be either matched rank-two spectral projector of the empirical separator and $P^*$ its population counterpart.  Then, after a possible block swap,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\norm{\wh P-P^*}_{F}}{\sqrt2}
 \le \frac{8\varepsilon}{\delta}.
 \label{eq:commutant-perturbation}
\end{equation}
If $\max_\ell\norm{\wh C_\ell-C_\ell}_{\mathrm{op}}\le\eta$ and
$\max_\ell\norm{C_\ell}_{\mathrm{op}}\le M$, one may take
$\varepsilon\le L(8M\eta+4\eta^2)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Davis--Kahan applied to the lowest eigenvector of the commutant Gram operator bounds the signed separator error by a constant times $\varepsilon/\delta$.  Applying it again to the two eigenvalue clusters of $H^*$, whose separation is one, gives \eqref{eq:commutant-perturbation}; the displayed constant is a conservative composition of the two bounds.  For the second statement, the commutator map satisfies
$\norm{[C,\cdot]}_{\mathrm{op}}\le2\norm{C}_{\mathrm{op}}$.
Expanding each empirical Gram term and summing gives
$L(8M\eta+4\eta^2)$.
\end{proof}

One contrast, a commuting within-block family, or equivalent blocks can enlarge the commutant kernel and destroy identification.  The code contains executable counterexamples for these cases.  The registered pilot also requires a cross-response eigengap, a commutant eigengap, and held-out off-block closure; a failed threshold is retained as a failed unit.

\subsection{Cross-fitted conditional covariance graph}

After fixing $Q_z$, define provisional residuals $R=Q_r^\top X$.  Centering can be included; in the exact benchmark the conditional means are zero.  For $i\ne j$, define the population edge weight
\begin{equation}
 S_{ij}=\Var\!\left(\E[R_iR_j\mid Z,C]\right).
 \label{eq:edge}
\end{equation}
The implementation estimates $\E[R_iR_j\mid Z,C]$ on the training split using a fixed nonlinear feature dictionary and evaluates predictive $R^2$ on validation data.  Test data are never used for the graph.

\begin{assumption}[Pair-block margin]
The residual coordinates admit a true partition $\mathcal P^*=\{\{i_1,j_1\},\ldots,\{i_m,j_m\}\}$ such that
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{\{i,j\}\in\mathcal P^*}S_{ij}
 \ge \sum_{\{i,j\}\in\mathcal P}S_{ij}+\gamma
 \label{eq:matching-margin}
\end{equation}
for every other perfect matching $\mathcal P$, for some $\gamma>0$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Pair-partition recovery on fixed residual axes]
Let $\wh S$ satisfy $\max_{i<j}|\wh S_{ij}-S_{ij}|\le\varepsilon$.  If the pair-block margin holds and $2m\varepsilon<\gamma$, then maximum-weight perfect matching under $\wh S$ recovers $\mathcal P^*$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For any matching $\mathcal P$, its empirical total differs from its population total by at most $m\varepsilon$.  Hence the empirical gap between $\mathcal P^*$ and any competitor is at least $\gamma-2m\varepsilon>0$.
\end{proof}

The theorem recovers a partition, not an orthogonal residual basis.  For blocks larger than pairs, the code uses a greedy score grouping.  That is a practical heuristic, not covered by this result.

\section{Algorithm}

\paragraph{Stage A: quotient discovery.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item Split data into train, validation, and test before model fitting.
\item Sample raw rectified-flow triples $(T,X_T,V)$ from train data and Gaussian source noise.
\item Estimate $\wh M=\frac1n\sum_i\operatorname{sym}(V_iX_{T_i,i}^\top)$ and take its leading $d$ eigenvectors as $\wh Q_z$.
\item Choose an arbitrary orthogonal residual completion $\wh Q_r$.
\item When conditional covariance contrasts satisfy the registered rank and commutant gates, recover an unordered residual block decomposition from \eqref{eq:cross-response}--\eqref{eq:commutant-gram}.  Otherwise, use fixed-axis matching only as a declared restricted model or retain a full residual covariance control.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Stage B: active flow.}
Transform each observation to $(Z,R)=\wh Q^\top X$.  Train a rectified-flow field $u_\theta(t,z,c)$ from
\begin{equation}
 \min_\theta\E\norm{Q_z^\top V-u_\theta(T,Q_z^\top X_T,C)}^2.
 \label{eq:active-loss}
\end{equation}
The population target is the active conditional velocity.  In the exact quotient class it closes in $(z,c)$.

\paragraph{Stage C: one-shot fiber.}
Train block-Cholesky functions $(\mu_\psi,L_\psi)$ by
\begin{equation}
 \min_\psi -\E\log\cN(R;\mu_\psi(Z,C),L_\psi(Z,C)L_\psi(Z,C)^\top).
 \label{eq:fiber-loss}
\end{equation}
All axes are retained; only covariance interactions are restricted to the declared blocks.

\paragraph{Stage D: generation.}
Sample $U,E$ independently from standard Gaussian, integrate the $d$-dimensional active ODE to get $Z$, sample $R=\mu_\psi(Z,C)+L_\psi(Z,C)E$ once, and decode $X=Q_zZ+Q_rR$.

\paragraph{Stage E: falsification.}
On held-out validation data, report active closure error, block-versus-diagonal fiber NLL, dimension sweeps, and total inference cost.  Increase $d$ or fiber complexity when the approximation error is larger than the predeclared compute penalty; do not claim a quotient when the residual field remains complex.

\section{Complexity}

Let $C_u(d)$ be one evaluation cost of the active vector field, $K$ the number of ODE evaluations, $C_f(D,d,b)$ the one-shot block-fiber cost, and $C_Q(D)$ the chart cost.  Then
\begin{align}
 C_{\mathrm{FIQ}}&=K C_u(d)+C_f(D,d,b)+C_Q(D),\label{eq:cfiq}\\
 C_{\mathrm{full}}&=K C_v(D),\label{eq:cfull}\\
 C_{\mathrm{latent}}&=K C_u(d)+C_{\mathrm{decoder}}(D,d).\label{eq:clatent}
\end{align}
For fixed small $b$, the block fiber is linear in the number of residual coordinates up to the context network.  The current dense orthogonal chart has $C_Q(D)=O(D^2)$ once.  Therefore:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item the \emph{iterative} complexity is exactly latent-dimensional;
\item total complexity has the same form as latent flow plus a one-shot decoder/fiber;
\item a proof of image-scale latent-order complexity additionally requires a structured chart with $C_Q(D)=O(D\log D)$ or $O(D)$, such as local lifting, wavelets, sparse couplings, or butterfly factors.
\end{itemize}
The experiments count the dense chart and fiber in wall-clock inference time; they do not claim orders-of-magnitude image speedups.
