# Padded Fourier bound review and separate synthetic checkpoint

The implemented padded Fourier bound is mathematically valid for the checked stride-one, odd-square-kernel, zero-padded convolution. Its FFT and SVD calculations use ordinary floating arithmetic and do not provide outward-rounded numerical bounds.

For an input grid of side $n$ and kernel radius $r$, embed each channel isometrically into the first $n$ sites of a torus of side $m=n+2r=n+k-1$, setting other entries to zero. At a retained output coordinate, each required shifted input index lies between $-r$ and $n-1+r$. A negative index wraps into sites beginning at $m-r=n+r$, outside the embedded input. Positive out-of-range indices also remain outside its support. Consequently, cropping the corresponding periodic operator reproduces the zero-padded finite-grid operator. Embedding has norm one and cropping has norm bounded by one.

The implementation places weights at centered offsets and applies an FFT. PyTorch uses cross-correlation, while this placement can be interpreted as the opposite convolution sign. This sign convention does not change the computed bound: reversing all offsets sends the frequency matrix at $\omega$ to the one at $-\omega$, permuting the complete frequency grid. For real weights it also corresponds to complex conjugation. Channel singular values and their maximum are unchanged. Transposing the array from channel-first FFT output to frequency-first channel matrices preserves the output/input channel order used by the singular-value calculation.

A unitary spatial Fourier transform decomposes the periodic channel operator into these frequency matrices. The usual unnormalized FFT of the kernel gives the correct convolution multipliers; no extra grid-size factor belongs in their spectral norm. The maximum frequency singular value bounds the compressed zero-padded operator. Taking the minimum of this bound and the independent spatial-offset Frobenius-sum bound remains valid in exact arithmetic. The helper's caller restricts the grid to positive size and the model to the stated convolution geometry. This reasoning does not cover arbitrary stride, padding, dilation, complex parameters, or grouped kernels without additional checks.
