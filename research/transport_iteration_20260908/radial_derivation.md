# Invertible radial GSM transport: derivation and implementation verification

This mathematical repair preserves the already specified GSM density. It introduces no new quality candidate and supplies no empirical evidence. No observed data were used.

Let phi_3 be standard Gaussian density and q(x)=sum_k w_k N_3(x;0,s_k^2 Omega), with positive weights/scales, positive-definite Omega, and det Omega=1. Write L for its positive-diagonal Cholesky factor. The whitened GSM is rotationally invariant. Its radius has CDF H(r)=sum_k w_k F_chi3(r/s_k), strictly increasing on r>0. For u!=0 define rho=||u||, r=H^{-1}(F_chi3(rho)), and T(u)=L u r/rho. Set T(0)=0. The inverse whitens x, sets r=||L^{-1}x||, and uses rho=F_chi3^{-1}(H(r)). Uniform direction and radius are independent in both laws, so the transform exactly pushes phi_3 to q in real arithmetic. It is one-to-one and onto.

The radial Jacobian determinant before L is (r/rho)^2 dr/drho, where dr/drho=f_chi3(rho)/H'(r). The identity is log|det DT|=log phi_3(u)-log q(T(u)). At zero, H(r)~c r^3 sum_k w_k s_k^{-3}, so r/rho tends to a=(sum_k w_k s_k^{-3})^{-1/3}; the Jacobian tends to a L and has determinant a^3. Smooth positive mixtures give the smooth radial extension; this statement holds for the exact mathematical transport. It does not establish smoothness of the finite bisection implementation.

Bounds min(s)*rho <= r <= max(s)*rho follow by stochastic ordering of component radii. Inverse bounds are r/max(s) <= rho <= r/min(s). The implementation bisects these finite brackets, reports iterations and maximum relative bracket width, and raises on failure to reach tolerance. It does not clip probabilities. For chi(3), Q(r)=exp(-r^2/2)[erfcx(r/sqrt(2))+sqrt(2/pi)r] gives stable log survival at radius 50 and beyond. For small r, F(r)=sqrt(2/pi)r^3/3 * 1F1(3/2;5/2;-r^2/2) gives stable log CDF even at 1e-100. The chosen smaller tail is compared in log space; mixture sums use log-sum-exp. Inputs outside supported floating-point range fail explicitly. Bracket-width guarantees assume the numerical special-function evaluations; they are not interval-arithmetic proofs of true-CDF rounding error. Density-ratio logdet inherits numerical inverse-CDF error and is not the exact derivative of a floating-point branching program.

Cost: each forward bisection step evaluates K component radial probabilities. For scale ratio kappa and relative tolerance tau, worst-case bisection iteration order is log2((kappa-1)/tau), in addition to O(K) density evaluation and fixed 3x3 linear algebra. Inverse bisection evaluates a single chi(3) law after one O(K) mixture target. This must be included in wall-clock and latency accounting. It does not retain a proven one-operation depth or constant-cost advantage over latent flow. The legacy many-to-one component-recycling sampler remains unchanged.
