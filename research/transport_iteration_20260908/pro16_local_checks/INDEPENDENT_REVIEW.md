## Algebra and comparison with the independent reflected kernel

The delivered positive-odds derivation is sound: with e the selected endpoint distance, c_e=1-e and normalized slopes s,l,r, the odds v=t/(1-t) satisfy s c_e v²+(l c_e-r e)v-s e=0. The two nonnegative numerator/denominator branches avoid cancellation, and independently evaluating t=N/(N+H), c=H/(N+H) avoids subtracting a rounded near-one root. The positive Bernstein denominator and derivative expressions give the stated analytic logdet. Common positive slope scaling preserves the real root. No projection of accepted roots is used. Positive real slopes alone do not guarantee representable discriminants or all subsequent finite arithmetic.

Our reflected kernel selects the orientation by the image of x-bin midpoint, targeting the short x-distance; Pro16 selects by the short y-distance and instead uses positive odds to obtain both complementary coordinates. Pro16 promotes **all** scalar work, including softmax and knot construction, to float64, then casts output/logdet back. Our kernel retains input dtype and original raw-to-knot rounding. These have the same ideal real family but are different floating programs, gradients, and training trajectories. The supplement's `reflected_only` helper is not our implementation: it uses the y-distance selector and original inverse coefficients.

Our source SHA is recorded in comparison.json. Both candidates accept all nine retained witnesses locally. Both accept the 32 extreme examples in this particular corpus. Relative to an independent 100-digit oracle on **our own realized knots**, our extreme maximum value/logdet errors are about `4.803e-7` / `6.040e-4`; Pro16's promoted-knot corpus has `1.166e-7` / `3.101e-7`. These are different realized knot maps and a small fixed corpus, not a uniform accuracy ranking. Comparing our outputs against Pro16's promoted-knot oracle instead gives a logdet discrepancy as large as 1.942; that cross-program difference must not be misreported as error against our own realized map.

Most importantly, **both candidates show rounded inverse-then-forward residual about 0.006 on the extreme corpus**, exceeding the native 0.001 coordinate gate. Pro16 checks oracle inverse accuracy there and explicitly records this round-trip failure; its `all_pass` for that subgroup does not enforce the native gate. Final rounding can destroy information in an ill-conditioned map even if the ideal inverse is accurately evaluated. No native gate may be waived on these receipts. A full backend change also requires new fair charged training/evaluation, not splicing repaired RQS continuation into an old comparison.

## Limits

All oracle checks use ordinary floating evaluation against high-precision bisection, not certified intervals. Finite gradients in fuzz banks do not prove correct parameter derivatives at every knot, whose logdet is generally only piecewise differentiable. No checkpoint, native observations, failed GPU batch, or model optimizer was accessed. The audit supports the algebra and reproduces several scalar mechanisms while retaining a failed portability assertion and an excessive round-trip diagnostic. It does not settle the native failure or qualify either backend.

The delivered package is already preserved separately in pro16_artifacts. This directory publishes all new replay payloads, scripts, and comparison receipts; COMPLETE_SHA256.json inventories the published files excluding itself. No binaries are omitted.
